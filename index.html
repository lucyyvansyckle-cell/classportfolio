About Me

Cybersecurity interests me because it combines technical problem solving with decisions that affect real people. I want to understand how a system works, where it could fail, and how to explain those risks clearly.

During two internships at General Dynamics Land Systems, I explored security technologies, tested solutions, and communicated findings to technical teams and stakeholders. I also served as Scrum Master for a cybersecurity team developing a Zero Trust product.

Those experiences helped me see how much security work depends on collaboration. A useful recommendation needs clear evidence, and a team needs shared priorities to turn that recommendation into a working solution.

Projects and Experience

Firewall Evaluation

I contributed to a build-versus-buy analysis of two firewall vendors during my 2026 cybersecurity internship. I tested configurations in an offline virtual machine and presented technical findings to stakeholders.

  • Compared two possible security solutions.
  • Tested configurations in an isolated environment.
  • Explained findings to support a selection decision.

Outcome: The recommended solution was selected for integration into the final product.

What I learned: Testing provides the evidence needed to make a technical recommendation useful to decision makers.

Post-Quantum Cryptography Research

During my 2025 internship, I researched PQC and contributed to a whitepaper. I also built a C++ and Rust prototype that simulated a PQC-encrypted Diffie-Hellman exchange between two Linux virtual machines.

  • Researched emerging cryptographic approaches.
  • Documented findings in Confluence.
  • Presented research and recommendations to the team.

What I learned: Prototyping helped me connect abstract cryptography concepts to the practical challenges of implementing a system.

Zero Trust Team Collaboration

I served as Scrum Master for a cybersecurity team developing a Zero Trust product. My work focused on helping the team organize priorities and translate security requirements into actionable tasks.

  • Facilitated sprint planning and daily stand-ups.
  • Supported backlog refinement and retrospectives.
  • Helped define user stories and acceptance criteria.

What I learned: Clear requirements and regular communication help a team identify obstacles and keep work moving.
